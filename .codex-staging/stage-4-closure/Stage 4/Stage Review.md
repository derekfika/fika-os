# Stage

Stage 4 — Business Decision Records and Pack Governance

# Purpose

Stage 4 turned approved business discovery into durable Business Decision Records and then into governed pack artefacts that could support downstream schema work without allowing implementation, provider behaviour or AI-generated convenience to define business meaning.

The current closure instruction states that the stage is complete. Earlier repository stage notes still describe Stage 4 as active human review; that is an evidence gap to synchronise in a later authorised repository update, not part of this closure task.

# What was achieved

Stage 4 established a repeatable path from canonical Decisions to reviewed BDRs, governed pack exports, schema drafts, fixtures, validation reports, traceability and repository integration evidence.

The strongest evidenced endpoint is Pack 3. Pack 3 records ten Service-domain BDRs, nine schemas, eighteen fixtures, zero schema validation failures, a Reflection, an Archive Certificate, canonical pack promotion and a repository integration report showing Pack 3 is ready for human review.

The wider stage also matured the workflow that Pack 1, Pack 2 and Pack 3 used: accepted decisions were preserved exactly, governed amendments were separated from explanatory edits, and repository write-back was separated from pack production.

# Biggest breakthroughs

The biggest breakthrough was the separation of authority from execution. Business authority remained with approved Decisions, Review Doctrine and the Authority Model. Codex could draft, stage, validate and report, but could not create business authority.

The second breakthrough was treating a pack as the governed artefact. Instead of handling individual BDRs or schemas as disconnected outputs, the workflow moved towards complete pack deliverables containing Markdown, schemas, fixtures, validation, traceability, manifest, reflection and archive evidence.

The third breakthrough was the use of valid and invalid fixtures as governance evidence. This made business boundaries testable without turning schemas into implementation designs.

# Biggest surprises

The workflow exposed how easily familiar terms can become unstable. Service, Service Arrangement, Service Occurrence, Scheduled Work, Operational Capability, Permission, Authority and Ownership all needed careful separation.

Another surprise was that the process itself needed governance. Pack 2 showed that earlier approved decisions could require governed refactoring once later Canon and Authority Model language became stronger. Pack 3 showed that the workflow needed Reflection and Archive Certificate artefacts to make pack closure complete.

Runtime limits were also visible. Full external JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 validation was unavailable during Pack 3 repository integration, so the evidence had to state clearly that local structural validation and pack validation evidence were used instead.

# Major workflow innovations

- Locked Decision wording and exact-text validation.
- Governed Decision/Register distinction between GDR and GRR.
- Frozen ZIP or canonical pack as the authorised run boundary.
- Staged pack production before repository integration.
- Pack-level Reflection and Archive Certificate.
- Canonical pack promotion before repository integration.
- Separate repository integration standard with manifest, diff and validation report.
- Error, warning and informational finding classification.
- Explicit disclosure when validation tooling is limited.

# Engineering lessons

Engineering work becomes safer when it starts from canonical business meaning rather than application behaviour. Schemas should represent business contracts, not database tables, APIs, UI models or provider payloads.

Validation has to be honest. If a full standards validator is unavailable, the report must say so and avoid overstating assurance.

Repository integration needs its own gate. Even when a pack is ready for archive, repository placement, filename collisions, link updates and exact-content checks still need a separate controlled workflow.

# Governance lessons

Governance worked best when it preserved history instead of rewriting it. Approved Decisions were kept distinct from explanatory sections, amendments were governed, and unresolved concepts stayed unresolved.

The stage reinforced that Ownership, Authority, Assignment and Technical Administration are separate concepts. It also reinforced that Business Meaning, Configuration, Capability and Permission must not be collapsed into each other.

The stage also showed that warnings are valuable. Informational warnings about external links, deferred terms or validator limits helped preserve trust without blocking progress unnecessarily.

# AI collaboration lessons

AI was most useful when it was bounded. Codex could accelerate reading, comparison, staging, schema generation, validation and reporting, but only inside the authority boundary set by humans and governed artefacts.

The strongest pattern was: human approval first, Codex processing second, validation third, human review fourth.

The workflow also showed that AI should preserve ambiguity rather than “clean it up” prematurely. Deferred terms such as Service Family, Service Template, Product, OPEXP and final fulfilment/work-record naming remained deferred because the evidence did not authorise resolution.

# Risks successfully avoided

- Business Decisions were not silently rewritten.
- Schemas were not treated as adopted implementation models.
- Provider or storage details were not allowed to define canonical business concepts.
- Repository integration was not collapsed into pack generation.
- Deferred concepts were not resolved without approval.
- Existing repository state was checked before integration.
- Pack source artefacts were verified as unchanged after integration.

# Things intentionally deferred

- Final resolution of Service Family, Service Template, Product, OPEXP and shared fulfilment/work-record naming.
- Full external Draft 2020-12 validation where tooling was unavailable.
- Repository status/documentation synchronisation for Stage 4 closure.
- Any implementation work, database design, API design or application refactoring.
- Any future amendments to accepted Decisions.

# Overall assessment

Stage 4 successfully moved FIKA OS from business discovery into governed business authority and pack-based downstream readiness. The stage did not merely produce documents; it produced a controlled operating model for turning approved business meaning into traceable artefacts.

The evidence is strongest for Pack 3 and for the workflow evolution through Pack 1, Pack 2 and Pack 3. Some early workflow history and exact dates are incomplete and should not be overstated.

# Recommendation for next stage

Begin the next stage with the pack workflow frozen. Future improvements should be considered after stage start only if they protect Canon integrity or remove a blocker that would otherwise prevent completion.

The next stage should focus on controlled repository review, schema adoption readiness and downstream implementation planning only where the relevant packs and business decisions provide sufficient authority.
