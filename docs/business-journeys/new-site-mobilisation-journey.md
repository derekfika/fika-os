# New-Site Mobilisation Journey

## Purpose and evidence

This is a concise senior-management view of the established MNK mobilisation process. It preserves the workbook's operational thinking without redesigning it. The workbook records responsibility mainly as FIKA, MNK or both; it does not establish permanent individual role ownership.

The current copy contains conflicting target dates: the main plan states a June 2026 go-live while the Questions/Actions tab retains an older April 2025 target. This appears to be a reused process artefact and requires version/date governance.

## Journey from contract award to launch

The workbook does not explicitly name phases. The following task families are an evidence-based summary for readability.

| Phase/task family | Current business work | Evidence of responsibility | Key dependencies/decisions | Human judgement preserved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Award and mobilisation alignment | Announce award, agree mobilisation plan, sign service agreement | MNK, FIKA or both | Contract award before shared plan and detailed delivery | Scope, expectations, trade-offs and relationship decisions |

## Mobilisation information streams

- **Task plan:** item, broad owner, completion percentage and timeline.
- **Questions/actions:** separate shell for questions, responses, deadlines and completion; currently empty in the inspected copy.
- **Notes:** physical/access constraints and supplier-account list.
- **Product lists:** barista/servery and kitchenette/pantry products, suppliers and some pricing/margin considerations.
- **First Orders:** initial quantities plus ordered/delivered flags.

## Recurring patterns worth preserving

1. Begin with joint FIKA/client alignment, not software setup.
2. Agree service expectations before completing products, prices and communications.
3. Walk the physical environment before final equipment decisions.
4. Select and sign off equipment jointly where client input matters.
5. Build staffing, compliance, supplier and physical-readiness streams in parallel.
6. Make first orders and delivery/unpacking visible before launch.
7. Include a launch week and early debrief rather than treating opening day as completion.

## Information duplicated across the workbook

- Supplier names appear in Notes, product lists and First Orders.
- Products and supplier relationships appear in both product lists and first-order tracking.
- Product pricing/offer decisions support tasks in the main plan but are not linked to them.
- Completion is represented through task percentage, open flags and ordered/delivered booleans.
- Go-live dates differ across tabs.
- Equipment selection, procurement, installation and training are separate rows without explicit dependency links.

## High-value administrative automation

Automation should support—not replace—the process:

- one controlled mobilisation version and target launch date;
- structured tasks with role, site/project scope, owner, deadline, status and evidence;
- dependency reminders and overdue/blocker escalation;
- questions/actions linked to the task that raised them;
- supplier-account onboarding and first-order status linked to one supplier/product reference;
- equipment selection, approval, order, delivery, installation, commissioning and training chain;
- staffing/recruitment/induction/training readiness summary without exposing unnecessary HR data;
- compliance/licence/training evidence and expiry tracking;
- opening-readiness summary and explicit go/no-go decision;
- launch/debrief actions carried into normal operations;
- leadership summary of progress, risk, decisions and exceptions.

## Work that requires human judgement

- service offering and expectations;
- commercial costings, supplier/product choices and substitutions;
- equipment suitability and client sign-off;
- recruitment and team suitability;
- risk assessments, safety acceptance and compliance sign-off;
- brand/signage/client communication decisions;
- operational-readiness and launch go/no-go;
- exception resolution and post-launch lessons.

## Journey conclusion

The existing process should remain the business backbone of a future Mobilisation module. The first improvement should be administrative clarity—ownership, dependencies, evidence, versioning and summaries—not replacement of Ed's mobilisation thinking.
