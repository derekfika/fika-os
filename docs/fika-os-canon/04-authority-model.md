# FIKA OS Authority Model

## Purpose

This model describes enduring authority boundaries in terms of organisational roles and functions rather than named people. It is governance guidance; exact approval rights for a business concept remain subject to its accepted BDR.

## Core rule

Authority belongs to an accountable organisational role or function, while named people receive time-bound assignments to exercise that authority.

A person's current seniority may be evidence of an assignment, but their name must not become the permanent governance rule.

## Business ownership

Business ownership is accountable for the meaning, purpose, scope and lifecycle of a business concept.

Business owners:

- approve or sponsor discovery questions within their remit;
- confirm business rules and resolve contradictions;
- nominate appropriate operational and subject-matter input;
- cannot use software behaviour as a substitute for a business decision.

Where ownership is not confirmed, record **TODO** and keep the decision or concept unadopted.

## Operational administration

Operational administration maintains approved catalogues, assignments and records within established rules.

Operational administrators:

- apply approved changes;
- maintain quality and completeness;
- follow required approval and history rules;
- do not gain authority to redefine canonical business language merely because they administer a catalogue.

Material additions, renames, retirements or changes with wider consequences require the applicable business approval. Exact Location Type administration remains subject to the governed TYPE-001 amendment.

## Commercial ownership

Commercial ownership is accountable for FIKA's commercial relationships, agreed commercial meaning and the business rules within its remit.

It does not own the independent identity of an OPLOC, a Client Contact or another domain merely because those concepts participate in a commercial relationship.

COMAG remains a discovery candidate, so its future ownership and approval boundary are not yet canonical.

## Platform governance

Platform governance protects cohesion across domains and delivery stages.

It:

- assesses cross-domain, reporting, migration and compatibility impact;
- prevents applications or providers from redefining canonical concepts;
- ensures downstream work traces to approved business authority;
- does not invent or approve business meaning on behalf of a business owner.

## Schema governance

Schema governance translates accepted business meaning into stable, reviewable contracts.

It:

- confirms traceability to accepted BDRs;
- checks ownership, identity, history, relationships and validation boundaries;
- prevents discovery candidates or implementation details from entering canonical schemas silently;
- cannot change a business decision to make a schema easier to design.

## Separation of authority

- Ownership, administration and approval are distinct responsibilities even when one role temporarily performs more than one.
- Administration authority does not imply publication or policy authority.
- Platform assessment does not replace business approval.
- Schema approval does not make an unresolved business concept canonical.
- Reporting may consume authoritative records but does not own their business meaning.
- Emergency or delegated authority must remain explicit, time-bound and auditable where later BDRs permit it.

## Governed change path

1. Evidence identifies a new need or conflict.
2. The accountable business owner leads discovery.
3. Human-approved wording becomes a canonical decision.
4. A BDR preserves the decision, rationale and consequences.
5. Platform and schema governance assess downstream impact.
6. Authorised administrators apply the approved change.
7. History and effective dates remain traceable.
