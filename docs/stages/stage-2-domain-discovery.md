# Stage 2 — Domain Discovery

## Purpose

Identify the business domains, journeys, terminology, evidence, ownership candidates and questions needed to understand FIKA OS.

## Business outcome

FIKA can describe the business landscape without allowing current applications, spreadsheets or providers to define business meaning.

## Inputs

- Stage 1 vision, scope and principles
- Repository and application inventories
- Current workflows, audits, workshops and business journeys
- Confirmed operational examples

## Core activities

- Discover domain boundaries and relationships.
- Separate canonical concepts from applications, projections, adapters and providers.
- Capture journeys, evidence and terminology.
- Identify candidate owners and unresolved business questions.
- Record uncertainty without inventing answers.

## Required artefacts

- Platform domain map
- Domain-discovery documents
- Business journeys
- Workshop material
- Evidence and repository audits

## Exit criteria

- Candidate domains and boundaries are documented.
- Evidence is traceable to its source.
- Open business questions are suitable for dependency-driven discovery.
- Historical terminology is distinguishable from proposed canonical terminology.

## Current status

**Complete.** The discovery evidence supported the 54-question business-discovery process.

## Dependencies on earlier stages

- Stage 1 — Vision

## Outputs consumed by later stages

Stage 3 uses the evidence and questions. Stages 4–6 use the domain map, journeys and retained evidence when explaining decisions and consequences.

## Out of scope

- Approving business answers
- Adopting schemas
- Selecting storage or application technology
- Implementing applications or integrations
